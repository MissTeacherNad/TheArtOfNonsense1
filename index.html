Step 02 · 10 minutes

Too important to be serious?

“Life is much too important a thing ever to talk seriously about it.”Oscar Wilde · Lady Windermere’s Fan · 1893

Read the quote twice. Discuss the apparent contradiction with your partner.

Step 03 · 5 minutes

A very official announcement.

Ministry circular no. 001“To stop students being late, we will remove all the clocks. Without an official time, nobody can officially be late.”

Read this announcement with a calm, serious voice. Pause after the first sentence.

Two useful ideas

Nonsense with an internal logic: the steps of a strange argument connect, even though its conclusion is absurd or impractical.

Deadpan humour: presenting something funny with a serious face and voice. En français : l’humour pince-sans-rire. Le flegme désigne plutôt le calme imperturbable.

One possible example

“To save paper, all tests must be imaginary. Students will answer in their heads, so we will never need to print anything.”

It saves paper, but the teacher cannot check anyone’s answers. Your rule should have its own surprising logic.

Our lesson recap

The picture presents an unusual, colourful character in a fantasy world. Wilde’s quote suggests that humour can help us look at life differently. Nonsense can follow its own logic. An absurd idea presented very seriously can make us laugh and think.

Our big question: How does British art turn madness into a creative power?

Later in the sequence

As Ministers of Nonsense, you will defend three absurd rules in a 3–4 minute presentation and answer a question from the audience. Today you are only trying out one rule.
